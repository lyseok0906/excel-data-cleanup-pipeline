# CleanSheetHQ Recommendations — Based on the 100-Site Monetization Pilot

## Context

CleanSheetHQ is a planned new English-language site in the niche "Excel Data Cleanup & Transformation" — covering text cleanup, numbers & dates, remove duplicates, Power Query cleanup, import & transform, and edge cases — differentiated via real Excel reproduction and screenshot evidence, currently pre-launch/pre-content. The judgments below draw on the Excel/Spreadsheet/Productivity batch (10 sites) as the primary comparator set, cross-referenced against patterns observed across the other 90 sites in the pilot, per `findings.md`. Every judgment below is evidence-based pattern observation from a 100-site pilot, not a guarantee of any specific financial outcome — the pilot's revenue data is thin (only 28 of 100 sites have a confirmed, non-UNKNOWN revenue figure) and every recommendation should be read with that uncertainty in mind.

## 1. Is the current "Excel Data Cleanup & Transformation" niche worth keeping?

Yes, on the evidence available. The niche has multiple documented, currently-operating sites earning real, confirmed revenue at moderate-to-small traffic scale: TrumpExcel (~91K/mo traffic, ~$10,000/mo revenue, evidence B), Spreadsheeto (~28.5K/mo traffic, ~$35,000/mo revenue as of 2020, evidence B), and Chandoo.org ("$1,000,000+" cumulative, evidence B, Mixergy interview). None of these required traffic anywhere near the millions/month that ad-dependent categories elsewhere in this sample (How-To Geek, The Spruce) needed to produce comparable outcomes, because the niche supports course, template, and add-in-sales monetization rather than requiring ad-RPM scale. "Data cleanup" specifically is a sub-slice of that broader Excel space that none of the 10 sampled Excel sites cover as their primary focus — ExcelJet, Contextures, and Excel Campus are formula/pivot-table-centric, and Ablebits/ExtendOffice are general add-in-tool-centric — suggesting there is room for a site that treats cleanup/transformation as its organizing principle rather than a secondary topic.

## 2. Is it too narrow / appropriately scoped / should it be widened?

The scope as stated (text cleanup, numbers & dates, remove duplicates, Power Query cleanup, import & transform, edge cases) is appropriately scoped, not too narrow. The pilot repeatedly shows that narrow, deeply-owned niches outperform broad ones on defensibility and are explicitly favored in the source batches' own transferability commentary: gHacks (Windows/browser/privacy only, 21 years, still ranking against far larger competitors), Windows Central ("own one narrow ecosystem completely" is called directly analogous to "CleanSheetHQ owning 'Excel data cleanup' completely"), and Whizlabs (25-year single-vertical certification focus) all validate narrow scoping over "cover everything in Excel." Contextures is the closest direct analog already in the sample — a single narrow slice of Excel (pivot tables/VBA) sustained for roughly two decades on evergreen SEO alone, rated Medium-High transferability specifically as "a good model for staying niche rather than expanding broadly." The recommendation is not to widen the initial scope, but to treat the six listed sub-areas as a complete, internally-clustered topic set (similar to ExcelJet's formula taxonomy or ExamTopics' per-entity template structure) rather than as a starting point to be expanded outward prematurely.

## 3. Is an initial target of ~30 articles appropriate?

Thirty articles is a reasonable minimum viable starting cluster but should be understood as the floor, not a sufficient endpoint, based on this sample. None of the 10 Excel/Spreadsheet sites in the pilot show meaningfully confirmed revenue at anything close to 30 articles — Spreadsheeto (the smallest confirmed-revenue site in that category) is described as having "~150+ articles," and even Contextures (weakest revenue evidence in the category) has "~200+ pages." Outside that category, ExamTopics-style per-entity template scaling and Zety's "1,400+ guides" job-title matrix both suggest that Excel-task-matrix content (one article per cleanup scenario x per data-type combination) can plausibly enumerate well beyond 30 distinct, defensible articles just within the six stated sub-areas. Thirty articles is appropriate as a first content sprint to validate the topic clusters and internal-linking structure (per ExcelJet's "strong internal taxonomy" success factor), but should be planned as phase one of a larger buildout rather than the target scale for meaningful traffic or monetization.

## 4. Is it better to move faster to 50-100 articles?

Yes, directionally, though this pilot cannot specify an optimal number. The confirmed-revenue Excel sites in the sample cluster in the 150-500+ article range (Spreadsheeto ~150+, TrumpExcel ~500+, ExcelJet ~500+), and the cross-category "rigid per-entity template repeated at scale" pattern (ExamTopics, Zety, Resume Genius) suggests that reaching the point where a site systematically covers every enumerable variant of its core task (every cleanup scenario, every common data-quality problem, every Power Query pattern) tends to require closer to 100+ pieces than 30. That said, this pilot did not measure the marginal return of article 31 through 100 directly for any Excel site, so 50-100 should be read as a directionally-supported next-phase target rather than a precisely evidenced number — the stronger evidence-based instruction is to prioritize completing the per-task matrix within the six sub-areas over hitting any specific article count.

## 5. Is ads-only the right initial monetization?

No. Across the full 100-site sample, ad-only monetization is the least differentiated and most scale-dependent path: of the 75 sites in the sample confirmed to run display ads, the majority have UNKNOWN revenue, and the sites that do rely primarily on ads (How-To Geek ~2.5M/mo, The Spruce ~4.4-13.3M/mo, Lifewire ~1.1M/mo) all required traffic scale far beyond what a pre-launch niche site can expect for years, if ever. Within the Excel category specifically, ExtendOffice (~304K/mo traffic, largely ad-supported, no confirmed revenue) and Contextures (revenue UNKNOWN, visitor-to-revenue distance explicitly rated HIGH "unclear monetization") illustrate that ad-centric Excel sites in this sample show weaker confirmed outcomes than course- or product-centric ones, even at comparable or higher traffic. Ads can reasonably be present as a small secondary layer once traffic exists, but should not be the primary or initial monetization plan.

## 6. Should affiliate be added from the start?

Cautiously, as a light secondary layer rather than a primary strategy, and only where genuinely relevant. Within the Excel category, affiliate presence is UNKNOWN or unconfirmed for most sites (ExcelJet, Contextures, Spreadsheeto, XelPlus, ExtendOffice, Chandoo.org all show UNKNOWN affiliate status), and where it is confirmed (Excel Campus, Vertex42 both D-graded/likely), it is not described as the primary driver anywhere in the category. Elsewhere in the sample, affiliate performs best when tied to genuinely high-commercial-intent, product-comparison content (AddictiveTips' VPN-affiliate niche, PCPartPicker's compatibility-then-retailer-click model) — a pattern that could apply to CleanSheetHQ only in narrow spots (e.g., comparing paid Excel add-ins or data-cleaning software), not across general tutorial content. Recommendation: keep affiliate opportunistic and limited to genuine product-comparison contexts from the start, but do not build the initial content plan around it.

## 7. Should a sample workbook/template be included?

Yes, this is one of the more strongly and consistently supported recommendations in the entire pilot. Vertex42 (rated High transferability: "proves downloadable, reproducible workbook assets alone... can build durable organic traffic — closely matches CleanSheetHQ's template/workbook angle") and Ablebits (free formula helper tool paired with a paid add-in, rated High: "pairing free educational content with a real downloadable tool/template mirrors Ablebits' trust-then-sell model") both directly validate this. Outside the Excel category, the same pattern recurs strongly: PCPartPicker's free compatibility tool (rated High: "the principle that free utilities/calculators build traffic and trust applies most directly to CleanSheetHQ"), Jobscan's free resume-diagnostic tool, and Remodelaholic's downloadable templates/printables shop (rated High: "selling downloadable Excel templates mirrors this exactly"). A sample workbook attached to each cleanup tutorial (showing before/after data, reproducible with the article's steps) would also directly support the stated "real Excel reproduction + screenshot evidence" differentiation strategy.

## 8. At what point should email capture be added?

From day one, not deferred. Of the 92 sites in the full sample with a confirmed answer on newsletter/email capture, 90 have it, making it one of the most universal traits in the entire dataset regardless of category, traffic scale, or monetization model — including sites as small as Spreadsheeto and as large as How-To Geek. Within the Excel category specifically, ExcelJet (100K+ subs), TrumpExcel (40K+ subs, described as "core to funnel"), and Chandoo.org (100K+ subs) all treat email as foundational infrastructure, not an add-on. Given that CleanSheetHQ's own product/course/template layer (per Q7 and Q9) will need a nurture channel to convert readers, email capture should be built into the site from its first published article rather than added once traffic exists.

## 9. Should a free Excel tool be built?

Yes, when feasible, but treat it as a phase-two investment rather than a launch-blocking requirement. The strongest single piece of supporting evidence is Ablebits (free online formula generator paired with paid add-in sales, rated High) and, cross-category, PCPartPicker's free compatibility checker (rated High, explicitly called the most directly applicable pattern for CleanSheetHQ in its source batch's own limitations note) and Jobscan's free resume-scan diagnostic tool (rated Strong: "free-diagnose-then-upsell mirrors a free spreadsheet-diagnostic tool"). A lightweight free tool (e.g., a browser-based or downloadable duplicate-finder, date-format fixer, or text-cleanup previewer) would function as both a lead magnet for email capture (Q8) and a natural next step from tutorial content into a demoable product (Q7), following the "content as tool demo" pattern seen in FluentU and Jobscan. This is not necessary for the first 30-50 articles but should be planned as an early-milestone investment once the core tutorial content exists.

## 10. Top 5 success patterns CleanSheetHQ should replicate first

1. **A single-answer, well-taxonomized reference structure for each discrete cleanup task**, following ExcelJet's model (rated High) — each article should answer one specific, exact-match search query completely, with a clear internal taxonomy linking related cleanup tasks together.
2. **A free downloadable workbook/template attached to every applicable tutorial**, following Vertex42 and Ablebits (both rated High) — reproducible, downloadable assets are the most consistently and strongly evidenced transferable pattern across this entire pilot, not just within the Excel category.
3. **Email capture built in from article one**, following the near-universal pattern (90 of 92 confirmed sites in the full sample) and the Excel category's own strongest performers (ExcelJet, TrumpExcel, Chandoo.org).
4. **Real screenshot/reproduction evidence as the trust mechanism**, mirroring the "tested and perfected" trust patterns seen in Once Upon a Chef and RTINGS.com's methodology transparency (both rated as strong trust-building analogs in their categories) — this directly matches CleanSheetHQ's own stated differentiation strategy and should be treated as a core execution requirement, not an optional nice-to-have.
5. **A course, template-pack, or tool-sale layer planned from the start as the primary monetization target, with ads/affiliate only as secondary layers**, following TrumpExcel, Spreadsheeto, and Chandoo.org (the three best-confirmed-revenue Excel sites in the sample, all course/product-centric) rather than the ad-dependent, high-traffic-requiring model of ExtendOffice or the general tech-tutorial sites elsewhere in the pilot.

## G. Research Protocol Revision Notes for a Future 1000-Site Study

**Fields that worked well.** Traffic estimates via Similarweb/Semrush (evidence B) were collectible for the large majority of sites and, despite cross-tool comparability limitations, provided a usable directional signal. Monetization-structure flags observed directly from the live site (evidence C — e.g., "does this page show a paywall," "does this page show display ad units," "is there a visible newsletter signup") were reliably collectible for nearly every site and proved to be the most consistently useful field category in the whole dataset. Winner-type classification and CleanSheetHQ-transferability judgments, while qualitative, were collectible for 100/100 sites and generated the most actionable content for this recommendations document.

**Fields that were effectively uncollectable (mostly UNKNOWN across the sample).** By name: `revenue_reported` (only 28 of 100 sites have a confirmed figure — the single weakest field in the dataset), `time_to_monetization` (UNKNOWN for the large majority of sites outside a handful of self-published income-report bloggers), `free_tool_calculator` (confirmed Y for only 9 of 79 sites with a known answer — either genuinely rare or under-detected), `saas_tool_lead` (Y for only 11 of 100, likely a genuine rarity rather than a collection gap), `sponsored_content` (only 58 of 100 sites have a confirmed answer at all, the lowest confirmation rate of any Y/N field in the schema), and `start_year`/`site_age` for many independent blogs predating clear "About page" documentation (frequently D-graded estimates rather than confirmed dates). A 1000-site study should expect these fields to remain thin unless a paid financial-data source or direct operator outreach is added to the methodology.

**Field definitions that were ambiguous.** `winner_type` categories overlapped in practice (e.g., "TRAFFIC WINNER" vs. "NICHE AUTHORITY" was a judgment call for several mid-traffic, long-tenured sites, producing the long tail of compound labels seen in this dataset's Counter output). `content_scale` was recorded inconsistently across batches (article counts vs. "large/medium/small" qualitative labels vs. self-reported cumulative lifetime stats like Vertex42's "170M visitors"), making cross-category numeric comparison difficult. `visitor_to_revenue_distance` (LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH) was a useful concept but was applied somewhat subjectively batch-to-batch without a shared operational definition of what counts as "low" versus "medium." A 1000-site study should pre-define these three fields with explicit decision rules before fielding.

**Fields plausibly automatable at scale vs. requiring human judgment.** Automatable via API-based tools: `traffic_est` (Similarweb/Semrush/Ahrefs APIs), `start_year`/domain-age (WHOIS or Wayback Machine first-capture APIs), and basic `display_ads`/`affiliate` presence detection (automated crawlers can detect ad-network script tags and common affiliate-link redirect patterns like `amzn.to`, `.sjv.io`, network cookies). Requiring continued human (or LLM-assisted qualitative) judgment: `winner_type`, `cleansheethq_transferability_summary` (or the equivalent transferability judgment for any target site), `why_it_works`/`key_success_factors` narrative synthesis, and any revenue figure that requires reading and evaluating the credibility of a specific interview or self-published report rather than pattern-matching a number.

**Concrete suggestions for a 1000-site study.** Add: a standardized "as-of date" field per traffic/revenue figure (this pilot recorded a rough Sept 2026 snapshot date but not per-field dates, making it hard to know how stale an individual number is); a structured "affiliate network detected" field (which specific networks, e.g., Amazon Associates, ShareASale, Impact) rather than a bare Y/N, since network choice is itself informative about commission structure; and an explicit "sample was curated, not random" flag repeated in the dataset itself (not just the methodology document) so downstream consumers of the CSV cannot mistake this for a representative sample. Remove or simplify: the free-text long-form fields (why_it_works, key_success_factors) as separate CSV-adjacent columns — they were valuable but consumed disproportionate compilation effort relative to their reuse, and at 1000-site scale would be better captured as a single structured tag list (e.g., ["income-transparency", "free-tool-lead-magnet", "flagship-data-asset"]) that can be tallied programmatically rather than read as prose per site.
